Framework

The "Why" Ladder

Every answer should survive 3 levels of "why."

Level 1 — What

"I used Random Forest."

Level 2 — How

"It handles non-linear relationships using bagging."

Level 3 — Why ✓

"My features had complex interactions. With 50K rows, bagging reduces variance without overfitting. And I needed feature importances for my non-technical stakeholder."

Live Demo

Same Question.
Two Answers.

"You built a model that detects fraud. Should you optimize for precision or recall?"

❌ Technically correct. Won't get the job.

"Precision is true positives over true positives plus false positives. Recall is true positives over true positives plus false negatives. For fraud, you'd want high recall."

→ Gave a definition when asked to reason.

✓ Shows reasoning, tradeoffs, business awareness

"The question is really about what type of mistake hurts more. A missed fraud: thousands lost, trust destroyed. A false alarm: customer gets a 'was this you?' text — annoying but not catastrophic. So I'd lean recall. BUT — at 10M transactions/day, high recall could mean 100K false alarms. I'd find the threshold where recall is high but volume is operationally manageable."

→ Reasoning + real-world tradeoffs + honesty about limits.

Framework

STAR-T Checklist

A checklist, not a script. Works for every stream.

Stands ForWhat to Say
SSituationWhat was the problem? Who cared?
TTaskWhat was YOUR specific role? Constraints?
AApproachWhat did you do, WHY, and what else did you consider?
RResultsWhat happened? Numbers. Honest assessment.
TTakeawaysWhat would you change? Limitations?

The Sentence That Changes Everything

"I chose X because Y, and I considered Z but it didn't fit because..."

The "Why" Across Streams

BA/DA: "I chose a waterfall chart over a stacked bar because the stakeholder needed to see sequential impact on revenue."

DS: "I chose XGBoost over logistic regression because feature interactions were non-linear. I considered a neural net but the dataset was too small."

DE/MLE: "I designed incremental ELT rather than full-refresh ETL because the table was 500M rows and full refreshes hit our 2-hour SLA."

AI/SWE: "I used RAG over fine-tuning because the knowledge base changes weekly and fine-tuning would require constant retraining."

Triad Pitches

7 min per person: 2 min pitch + 3 min follow-ups + 2 min feedback

"Why that approach and not [alternative]?"
"What would happen if the data changed?"
"How do you know that metric was good enough?"
"What was the biggest limitation?"
"How would this work at scale / in production?"
"How did you communicate results to stakeholders?"

Can't answer a follow-up? Write it down. That's your study list.

During the Interview

→ Pause for 5 seconds to think. Interviewers respect it.
→ "I'm not certain, but here's how I'd reason about it..." is a great answer.
→ Asking a clarifying question shows you think before you act.
✕ "I don't know" + silence is NOT OK. Always offer your thinking process.
